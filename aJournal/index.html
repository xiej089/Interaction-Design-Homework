A WALK THROUGH NEW YORK CITY

SUNLIGHT
/ UNDERGROUND

A journey from First Avenue to Parsons, recorded through warmth, sound, and small things.

01

FIRST AVENUE

The first thing I noticed was the sunlight. It came out the buildings and landed directly on my face as I stepped outside.

The air was cool, but the sun made my shoulders feel warm. For a moment, I wanted to walk more slowly.

Before heading toward Grand Central, I stopped at the coffee shop downstairs and bought an iced Americano. The plastic cup was cold against my hand.

Condensation slowly collected around my fingers, while the rest of my body stayed warm in the sun.

warm.

02

MIDTOWN

Midtown was already awake.

Men in dark suits walked quickly past me. Some held coffee. Some stared at their phones. Some were already talking into their AirPods.

step. step. step. step.

At the corner, flowers sat outside a small shop. Pink, yellow, white, green.

For a few seconds, they looked strangely soft against the gray buildings.

Above them were advertisements of every size: giant screens covering buildings, posters taped onto construction walls, and small handwritten signs in shop windows.

EVERYONE IS BUSY LIVING.

03

SMALL THINGS

A bagel shop on the corner.

A yellow taxi stopping too suddenly.

A delivery bike rushing past me.

A man adjusting his tie while waiting for the light.

A pile of black garbage bags.

Flowers wrapped in clear plastic.

Steam rising from the street.

My coffee slowly melting into ice water.

Someone saying, "Excuse me."

A siren somewhere far away.

04

GRAND CENTRAL

The closer I got to Grand Central, the louder the city became.

Car horns overlapped with voices, footsteps, engines, and construction.

Then I went underground.

The sunlight disappeared almost immediately.

The air felt heavier. Warmer. Metallic.

My footsteps became part of hundreds of other footsteps moving through the station.

BEEP.

BEEP.

BEEP.

05

THE PLATFORM

I could hear the train before I could see it.

SCREEEEEEEEEECH

Wind rushed through the platform before the train arrived.

The headlights appeared inside the tunnel.

Everyone moved slightly closer to the edge without really noticing they were doing it.

06

4 TRAIN

Inside the train, everything became compressed.

Shoulders almost touched.

Someone held the pole with one hand and scrolled through their phone with the other.

Someone was asleep.

Someone's headphones were loud enough that I could hear the beat.

The train suddenly moved, and everyone's body leaned in the same direction for half a second.

RRRRRRRRRRRRRR

Darkness outside the window.

Lights.

Darkness again.

My reflection appeared in the glass for a second and disappeared.

"14 Street–Union Square."

Everyone stood up at once.

07

UNION SQUARE

When I walked out of Union Square station, the sunlight felt surprisingly strong.

After the darkness of the subway, everything looked brighter than before.

Trees moved in the wind.

People sat on benches.

Someone carried groceries.

Someone walked a dog.

Someone was already late.

THE CITY HAD CHANGED WHILE I WAS UNDERGROUND.

08

PARSONS

I walked toward Parsons with the last few sips of my Americano.

The ice had mostly melted.

The cup was no longer cold.

The sunlight was still warm.

The sounds of the city became ordinary again.

I stopped listening.
